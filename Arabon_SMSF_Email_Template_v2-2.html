Most SMSF property purchases that go wrong don't fail because of the property. They fail because of what wasn't in place before the offer was made.

Here's the part that catches people out.

Borrowing through super doesn't work like a normal mortgage

Your SMSF can't just take out a loan. The one route available is a Limited Recourse Borrowing Arrangement, or LRBA:

A separate holding trust is set up to hold legal title to the property while the loan is outstanding
Your SMSF trustee borrows the funds and becomes the beneficial owner, collecting the rent and any capital growth
If the loan defaults, the lender's claim is limited to that one property, not the rest of your fund
Once the loan is repaid, legal title transfers to the SMSF

Straightforward in theory. In practice, the holding trust needs to exist before a property goes under contract, not after, and this is the single most common step people miss.

What's changed, and what hasn't

New residential LRBAs are no longer available. If you were planning to gear into a house or unit through your fund, that path has closed. What's still fully open is borrowing for business real property, commercial premises used wholly and exclusively in a business. If your fund is buying a house, it's cash only now. If it's buying commercial, borrowing is still on the table.

Why the finance side is where things stall

SMSF lending is a specialist category. Fewer lenders offer it, the criteria are tighter, and the loan has to be structured to match the LRBA exactly from day one.
